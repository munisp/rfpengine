# Source findings: certification scope and group resources

Research date: 2026-10-12. These findings inform a software design, not a legal determination for any company.

IAF MD 1:2023 §§3.3.1 and 5 permit a multi-site management-system organization to span multiple legal entities, but sites must have a legal/contractual link with the central function and operate under a single management system with central authority, review and internal audits. §§4.3 and 6.1.1.3 make important boundaries explicit: broader legal considerations are not covered by this auditing document, and scheme-specific requirements can take precedence. Parent ownership alone therefore cannot establish coverage or procurement acceptability. [1]

FAR 9.104-3(a) normally requires acceptable evidence of ability to obtain resources, usually a commitment or explicit arrangement; §9.104-3(c)(1) normally treats affiliates as separate entities for responsibility determinations. This supports explicit resource commitments, not presumed group pooling. It does not transfer a certificate or resolve the entire solicitation's requirements. [2]

13 CFR 121.103(a)(6) generally counts the subject concern and its domestic/foreign affiliates when determining size. The section contains exceptions. Do not inherit a parent's small-business/program status or assume a subsidiary's standalone headcount establishes eligibility. Apply the actual program and solicitation rules. [3]

UKAS CertCheck provides verification and tracking of claims of UKAS-accredited management-system certification. A validity check is one input; entity/site/activity scope and procurement applicability still need evidence. Its public service is not evidence that BidForge has an authorized API integration. [4]
